# What is a Digital Scholarly Edition?

## Summary

In the chapter «What is a Scholarly Digital Edition?», Patrick Sahle establishes the fundamental criteria to define a true Scholarly Digital Edition (SDE), distinguishing it sharply from simple digitization practices.

The key points of his theory are:
    • SDE vs. Digitization: Sahle contrasts the Scholarly Digital Edition (SDE) with the digitized edition (e.g., a static PDF). A true SDE must follow a «digital paradigm» and not merely reproduce the printed page on a screen.
    • The Print Test: The distinctive feature is that an SDE cannot be printed without a significant loss of information or functionality. If an edition can be printed without losing its essence, it is not truly digital but merely «digitized».
    • Critical Representation: The edition must not just be an archive of images («facsimiles»), but must offer a «critical representation» based on rigorous rules, integrating analysis tools («analytic tools») and interconnections impossible on paper support.

In summary, Sahle defines the digital edition not by the medium, but by its capacity to transcend the physical limits of the traditional book
