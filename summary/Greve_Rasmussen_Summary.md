# Reading or Using a Digital Edition?

## Summary

Krista S. G. Rasmussen analyzes the different roles a user adopts when engaging with a scholarly digital edition, distinguishing between reading and using.

Her key points are:
    • Reader vs. User: Rasmussen identifies three roles: the reader (interested in a reliable text for continuous reading), the user (who studies the text, variants, and uses search tools), and the co-worker (who contributes data).
    • Knowledge Sites: Digital editions are often «knowledge sites» (Shillingsburg), offering vast data. However, this richness can disrupt «phenomenological immersion» due to the constant «urge to click».
    • Text vs. Work: While the reader seeks the «work», the user focuses on «documents» and intertextual relations.

In conclusion, a well-designed digital edition must balance critical depth with readability, acknowledging that users shift between these roles.
