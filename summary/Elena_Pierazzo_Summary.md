# Modelling Digital Scholarly Editing: From Plato to Heraclitus

## Summary

In this chapter, Elena Pierazzo analyzes the fundamental theoretical shift in textual scholarship from a Platonic view (text as a stable, ideal object) to a Heraclitean view (text as flux) within the digital medium.

The key points of her argument are:

- Heraclitean Model: Pierazzo argues that digital texts are intrinsically mutable, embracing the «mutability» of text rather than trying to fix it into a single authoritative version («one-text paradigm»).

- Paradigmatic Editing: She proposes editions that treat textual variation as a defining feature, not an error. The digital environment allows editors to represent the «process aspects» of writing (genesis and evolution) far better than print.

- Editorial Implications: This shift requires new models that do not hide textual complexity but make it accessible. It challenges the traditional hierarchy between the reading text and the critical apparatus.

In summary, Pierazzo invites scholars to view mutability not as a flaw, but as a core characteristic of digital textuality.
