# Literary Documents, Texts, and Works Represented Digitally

## Summary

Peter Shillingsburg examines the tension between the archival and editorial impulses in the digital environment.

The salient points are:
    • Archive vs. Edition: Shillingsburg distinguishes between the archive (which reiterates and preserves historical documents) and the edition (which creates new, critical texts). The digital favors the archive, but should not exclude critical editing.
    • Text as New: Every edition, print or digital, produces a new text. Digital publishing is not mere reproduction but a «representation» subject to reconfiguration.
    • Tools for Scholars: He laments the lack of user-friendly tools for scholars who are not programmers. Technology must "speak the language of humanists" to enable the creation of true critical digital editions.

In summary, Shillingsburg defends the editor's right and duty to intervene critically, rather than limiting work to digital facsimiles.
